California's Changing Landscape

California Wildfires

Wildfires are a natural part of California's environment, but extreme heat, dry vegetation, wind, and human activity can cause fires to grow quickly and threaten communities.

Overview

Fire is part of California's story

California has forests, grasslands, mountains, deserts, and communities that can all experience wildfires.

Learning how fires start, how they spread, and how people can prepare can help communities stay safer.

Quick Facts

Six things to know about California wildfires

Dry vegetation burns easily

Grass, shrubs, branches, and trees can become very dry, which makes it easier for wildfires to start and spread.

Wind can make fires spread faster

Strong winds can push flames forward and carry burning embers into new areas.

People can start wildfires

Some wildfires begin because of human activity such as equipment, vehicles, campfires, or power lines.

Wildfire smoke can travel far

Smoke from wildfires can spread far beyond the fire itself and affect air quality in other communities.

Wildfires affect wildlife

Fires can damage habitats and force animals to move away from areas where they normally live.

Preparation can help communities

Emergency plans, evacuation routes, alerts, and defensible space can help people prepare for wildfire danger.

The Impact

Wildfires affect more than the land they burn

Communities

Wildfires can damage homes, force evacuations, and disrupt neighborhoods and local businesses.

Air Quality

Smoke can travel long distances and make the air unhealthy, even in places far away from the fire.

Wildlife

Fires can destroy habitats and force animals to move into different areas to survive.

Infrastructure

Roads, power lines, and other public services can be damaged or shut down during major wildfires.

Be Prepared

Staying safe before and during a wildfire

Know your evacuation route

Learn more than one way to leave your neighborhood in case a road is closed during an emergency.

Pay attention to alerts

Follow local emergency alerts and instructions so you know when it is time to evacuate.

Prepare important supplies

Keep water, medications, important documents, and other emergency supplies ready ahead of time.

Create defensible space

Clearing dry vegetation and other flammable materials around a home can help reduce wildfire risk.
